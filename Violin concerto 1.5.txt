<p align="center"><em>Allegro con brio, alla festa; con una voce sotto la musica</em></p>
<p align="center">***</p>

<p>The interview room had a backdrop with the sponsors on it and two chairs angled at the friendly angle, and the woman across from Sasha was good at her job, which meant the questions would be gentle and there would be no way out of any of them.</p>

<p>"So. The comeback season. Let's start with the obvious one: what are you here for?"</p>

<p>"Gold." Sasha smiled the smile that photographed well. "Same as always. I don't know another reason to lace skates. If I'm on the ice, I'm there to win, and I'll fight for it with everything I have. Anything it takes."</p>

<p>"Some things never change."</p>

<p>"Some things never change," Sasha agreed, and reached for the water, and her sleeve pulled back off her wrist, where a black elastic sat that nobody in the room would ever think to ask about.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>The gala had been the organizers' idea for about eleven seconds, which was how long it took Sasha to make it hers.</p>

<p>They had come to her first, because you always come to the loud one first, with the proposal laid out in the language of such proposals: an anniversary show, a full arena, the whole Khrustalny generation on one ice, and, here the man's voice had gone delicate, perhaps, if the girls were open to it, a moment the audience had been waiting years for. The two of them. Together. A duet. He had said the word rivalry and then apologized for it in the same breath, the way people had been doing for three years.</p>

<p>He had brought a folder of ideas. Sasha did not open the folder.</p>

<p>"Yes," she said. "On one condition. I build it."</p>

<p>"You... the choreography?"</p>

<p>"All of it. The music, the program, every step. You get your moment, the crowd gets their moment, and nobody touches it but me." She had smiled at him then, warm as a stadium, and the man had gone away delighted and slightly uncertain what he had agreed to, which was the correct amount of uncertain, because what he had agreed to was this: that if the world was going to demand a performance of the two of them, then the person holding the pen would be the person with something to write.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>"You and Shcherbakova. The story that will not die." The interviewer leaned in, comfortable, sympathetic, a professional at the door of a private house. "Three years of headlines. The duel, the quad war, all of it. And now you're skating a duet at the anniversary gala. So let me ask what everyone will want asked. What are you two, really?"</p>

<p>"Friends."</p>

<p>"Friends."</p>

<p>"We're friends." Sasha held the smile at exactly the weight it was built for. "People made a war out of two girls from one rink because a war sells better than a rink does. Fine, that's the job, I stopped arguing with headlines when I was fifteen. But you asked what we are." She turned the water bottle one quarter turn on the table, and set it down. "We are friends."</p>

<p>The interviewer nodded, and made the small note interviewers make, and the word went into the file with all the other words, true, verified, printable, and the room moved on to the next question, and nobody in it heard the sentence the way she had said it to herself at four in the morning in a kitchen, which was as the largest sentence she was permitted to carry in public, and the only one, and she had learned to pack everything she owned inside it.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>The rehearsals were closed. That had been the other condition.</p>

<p>Anna arrived at the first one with her bag on her shoulder and her guards in her hand, and stood at the boards reading the printed sheets Sasha had sent over, three pages of structure in aggressive capitals, and read them the way she read everything, completely, twice, without commentary.</p>

<p>"Slow," she said at last.</p>

<p>"You've complained for eleven years that I skate too fast to see."</p>

<p>"I never complained. I observed." Anna set the pages down on the boards and stepped out of her guards. "It's a good build. The second half especially." She pushed off, unhurried, reading the ice now instead of the paper. "You wrote this for two people who know each other."</p>

<p>"I wrote it for us. Same thing."</p>

<p>"Mm," said Anna, in the tone of a person filing something for later, and skated to her mark.</p>

<p>They built it across three weeks of early mornings, before the building woke, the hour that had always belonged to them even in the years when neither of them would say so. And it came together the way Sasha had known it would and could never have explained to the man with the folder: fast where the crowd needed fast, one landed jump apiece because the posters demanded it, and underneath all of that, running the whole length of it like a watermark, a slower thing, built out of eleven years of accumulated knowledge of exactly how another person moves. Where Anna breathed. How Anna took a pivot. What Anna's free hand did when she trusted the ice, which was open, and what it did when she did not, which was nothing at all.</p>

<p>It was in the fourth rehearsal that Anna stopped, mid-run, at the far turn, where the music gathered itself and waited.</p>

<p>"What's here?"</p>

<p>Sasha glided in from her line. "Where?"</p>

<p>"Here. Bar before I cross behind you. Your wrist does this," and Anna reproduced it exactly, of course she reproduced it exactly, the turn with the small wait built into it, the delay the width of a person, "and then nothing happens for half a count before I arrive. It feels like a door held open too long. Like I'm arriving late to something."</p>

<p>The rink was very quiet. The morning shift of two, and the hum in the roof, and eleven years, and Sasha looked at the space between her own wrist and the girl standing in it, and answered in the only language she had ever been fluent in.</p>

<p>"You're not late," she said. "You're arriving exactly on time. Trust the pause. It knows what it's doing."</p>

<p>Anna looked at her for a moment, the way she looked at a score she suspected of containing more than its notation. Then she nodded, once, and skated back to her mark, and did not ask again, and from that morning on she came through the space with a precision that had nothing casual in it, the way you handle an object whose value you have not been told and have begun to guess.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>"Last one, and then I'll let you go. Beijing." The interviewer said it gently, the way you put a cup down on glass. "The year after. The retirement that wasn't. You've never really talked about what brought you back. So, what did?"</p>

<p>Sasha was quiet for a moment, which the camera would read as composure.</p>

<p>"I missed the ice," she said. "That's the boring answer, and it's true. But it's not the whole of it." She turned the bottle another quarter turn. "There was a while when I wasn't skating, and somebody kept showing up anyway. That's all I'll say about it. Not a coach, not the federation. Just, every day, the same time, whether I wanted it or not. And you can outrun almost anything, I'm very fast, ask anyone." The room laughed; she let it. "But you can't outrun something that isn't chasing you. It just waits for you to get tired. So I got tired." She smiled, and the smile was different for exactly one frame, and no editor would ever find it. "And then I laced up."</p>

<p>"Whoever they are, they sound like a good friend."</p>

<p>"The best I have," Sasha said. "That's on the record. Print that part."</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>The arena on the night was full past the flags, and the gala ran long and golden, one generation after another taking the ice to its own era of music, and the flashes came in scattered handfuls, here, there, a lit crackle running the bowl whenever a name went up.</p>

<p>They were the last number before the finale. Of course they were; the organizers were sentimental, not stupid.</p>

<p>The announcer took his time with it. He named them separately first, the titles, the records, the whole apparatus, and the building rose separately for each name, and then he said the two names together in one sentence, and the sound that came down was not applause, it was weather, and into the middle of that weather two girls in matching dark blue skated out from opposite gates, met at center ice, and took hands.</p>

<p>The flashes went from handfuls to volleys.</p>

<p>Friends, said the commentators, in six languages, over the opening bars. The famous rivals, together at last. What a moment for the sport. And the whole arena agreed, eighteen thousand people holding up their phones to film a reconciliation, a story with a shape they knew, the war ending, the medals forgiven, the two girls from one rink with their hands intertwined the way friends' hands intertwine, and every word of it was true, and every camera in the building was pointed at the truth, and the truth stood on the ice in matching blue and performed itself at full brightness for the people it belonged to.</p>

<p>The program ran fast where Sasha had built it fast. The crowd got its jump, and its second jump, and the volleys crackled, and the noise rode up and up, and then the music gathered itself at the far turn and waited, and the arena, sensing a change of weather without knowing why, quieted by degrees, eighteen thousand people leaning forward into a slow section they had not been promised.</p>

<p>Sasha's wrist turned.</p>

<p>The wait opened in it, half a count, the width of a person, the door held exactly as long as it had been held in an autumn hotel room and a February arena and four hundred solitary run-throughs, the space that had traveled three years with a false name on its label, and into that space, on time, exactly on time, came Anna, crossing behind and through, her hand finding the held hand, her line completing a shape that had been performed its whole life as a fragment, and for four seconds, in front of eighteen thousand witnesses and every camera in the northern hemisphere, the step existed as it had been written.</p>

<p>Nobody saw it. That is not correct: everybody saw it, the whole building saw it, it was the moment half the arena chose for their video, the two of them moving as one thing through the slow turn with the flashes going off like a lit sea. Everybody saw it and nobody read it, because the world had been handed the one story it wanted, friends, and a story that satisfying uses up all the light around it, and the truer thing traveled through the middle of the noise the way it had always traveled, in the open, in plain sight, addressed to no one, signed with a wrist.</p>

<p>Anna came through the turn and out of the shape, and her hand left Sasha's hand at the choreographed beat, not before it, and the fast section took them both back up into the brightness, and the number ended the way galas end, spinning, arms up, the roar coming down like a roof.</p>

<p>They bowed together at center ice, hands joined and lifted, the picture the organizers had dreamed of, the picture the papers would run, the picture that was, everyone agreed, worth the ticket alone: two friends, restored to each other, laughing in the flash-storm.</p>

<p>Sasha was laughing. That was the thing about a compulsory festival that nobody warns you about, that the joy in it is real, that you can hold a person's hand in front of the entire world under a banner reading exactly one word less than the truth, and the hand is still warm, and the roar is still a roar, and four seconds of a completed step is still four seconds more than the drawer ever paid out before. She had chosen this. Standing in the noise with her arm raised and her fingers laced through the fingers of the girl half a head shorter, she chose it again, deliberately, the way you re-sign a contract you have read every line of: the permitted version, performed at full volume, forever if necessary. It was not everything. It was not nothing. It was what there was, and she was the one holding the pen, and nobody in the building but her would ever know how much of the truth had just been played at them, at the top of its voice, disguised as a gift.</p>

<p>The announcer's voice came up over the roar, calling them back for the second bow.</p>

<p>Anna glanced at her, breathing hard, flushed with the noise, one strand come loose from all the turning, and said, under the music, at a volume designed to reach one person and no further:</p>

<p>"Your left side's coming loose."</p>

<p>"I know," Sasha said. "Leave it."</p>

<p>And they went back out into the light, hand in hand, as the arena stood, and the flashes rose to meet them, and the festival, which never ends, began its encore.</p>
