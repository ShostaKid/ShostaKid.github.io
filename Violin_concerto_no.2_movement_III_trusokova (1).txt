<p align="center"><em>III. Scherzo: allegretto, ma sempre fuori tempo; con trio, e da capo</em></p>
<p align="center">***</p>

<p>Kamila Valieva was thirteen years old and had a system.</p>

<p>The system was this: get to the rink forty minutes before her own group, sit at the boards at the end where the juniors were not supposed to sit, and watch Anna Shcherbakova work.</p>

<p>She had never told anybody about the system. There was nothing to tell. She was not stealing anything, and she asked permission for the questions she asked afterward, and she asked a great many of them. What she had worked out, at eleven, was that Anna's skating had a clock in it. Every entry arrived at exactly the moment the music said it should, every time, at every run-through, in practice or in competition or at nine in the morning with nobody in the building. You could set your own body to her. Kamila had spent two years setting her own body to her, counting under her breath at the boards, and it had made her better, and nobody had ever noticed her doing it.</p>

<p>So on Sunday morning she was at the boards at ten past eight with her guards on and a bottle of water she had forgotten to drink, counting.</p>

<p>One and. Two and. Three and.</p>

<p>Anna went into the step sequence a full beat late.</p>

<p>Kamila stopped counting and started again from the top of the phrase, because obviously she herself had come in wrong. She had not been listening properly. She listened properly. One and, two and, and Anna came out of the twizzle a half-beat early to compensate for the late entry, which meant she was now wrong in the other direction, and she carried that wrongness into the transition and had to hurry three steps to catch the phrase before the jump.</p>

<p>She did not catch it.</p>

<p>The takeoff was rushed. What was supposed to be a triple lutz went up as a single, one lonely rotation in a body that had been asking for three, and Anna landed it and put a hand out and skated away from the whole thing with her face absolutely blank.</p>

<p>Kamila sat at the boards with her mouth slightly open.</p>

<p>Anna Shcherbakova did not pop jumps. Other people popped jumps. Kamila herself popped jumps and was shouted at for it. Anna's whole reputation in that building rested on the fact that whatever went into the air came down having done what it was told, and Kamila had watched her for two years and had seen it happen perhaps four times, all of them in the week she had come back from the ankle.</p>

<p>She popped two more before nine o'clock.</p>

<p><em>Fuori tempo.</em></p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>By half past nine it had stopped being an interesting problem and had become something Kamila did not want to look at.</p>

<p>The run-through fell apart in the second half. Anna two-footed a landing that Kamila had never once seen her put a second foot on. She went into a combination spin and lost the count somewhere in the middle of it, which meant she came out of it before the music did, and then stood there on the ice for a full second with nowhere to go and nothing to do, in front of everybody.</p>

<p>Eteri said something short from the boards. Anna nodded and went back to the top.</p>

<p>The second attempt was worse.</p>

<p>What frightened Kamila, and it was fright, she would have used that word if anybody had asked her, was not the errors. It was the face. Anna Shcherbakova's competition face was a famous object; you could put a camera four feet from it after a disaster and get nothing at all. This was not that face. This was somebody who had left the building and forgotten to take her body with her. Between run-throughs she stood at the boards with her water bottle in her hand and did not drink from it, looking at a point on the ice about three metres in front of her, and once, when the choreographer said her name twice, she did not hear either of them.</p>

<p>And on the far side of the rink, at the same time, something completely different was going on.</p>

<p>Sasha Trusova was having the best morning of her life.</p>

<p>She landed the quad toe first thing, before her muscles were properly warm, out of a step that had no business supporting it. She landed it again twenty minutes later. She landed a salchow so clean that a coach from the other group came over to the boards to watch the next one, and she landed the next one too. Her spins were centred. Her step sequence arrived exactly where the music put it. The Russian Rocket, who fell over more times in a normal Tuesday than most of that building fell over in a month, went through three hours of Sunday practice without going down once.</p>

<p>And she was loud. Kamila had known Sasha for two years, and Sasha was always loud, but this was something else again. She shouted encouragement at juniors she had never spoken to. She had an argument with the sound technician about the volume of somebody else's programme and won it. She sang. She skated backward across the entire rink while singing, at speed, and was told off, and apologised extravagantly, and did it again eleven minutes later.</p>

<p>Kamila counted her, out of habit, and found that she could.</p>

<p>That was the thing nobody else in the building would have noticed, and Kamila noticed it because counting was what she did. Sasha was on the beat. Sasha, who lived a fraction ahead of every piece of music she had ever been given, who attacked everything early because waiting was a foreign language to her, was sitting exactly inside the phrase this morning like a girl who had nowhere else she needed to be.</p>

<p>The two of them had swapped. That was the only way Kamila could put it to herself at thirteen. Her two reference points had traded places overnight, and nobody else appeared to have noticed, and she sat at the boards holding a water bottle she had still not opened.</p>

<p>There was one more thing.</p>

<p>In three hours, they did not speak to each other once.</p>

<p>Not one word. They passed within two metres four separate times. Sasha talked to everybody in that building, including the man who fixed the barrier padding, and she did not talk to Anna. She did not look at her, either, except once, and Kamila only caught it because she happened to be looking in the right direction at the right moment: Anna went past on a crossover, and Sasha's eyes came up off the ice and followed her the whole way, and then went back down.</p>

<p>She was smiling. That was the part Kamila could not make sense of at all. Whatever had happened, and something had obviously happened, Sasha Trusova was in the middle of it and enjoying herself enormously.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p>Kamila went and asked her, because at thirteen you can still simply go and ask people things.</p>

<p>She waited until Sasha came off for water and got herself into the space beside her at the boards, and said, in the voice she used for asking questions of seniors, which was polite and quiet and about forty per cent smaller than her real one:</p>

<p>"Sasha? Is Anya all right?"</p>

<p>Sasha lowered the bottle. "What makes you ask that, Kamilochka?"</p>

<p>"She's popped three jumps."</p>

<p>"Has she."</p>

<p>"And she came out of the combination early, and she two-footed the flip, and she's late into everything, and she's never late into anything, she's the one who's never late into anything." Kamila heard her own voice climbing and pulled it back down. "And she hasn't talked to you all morning, and you haven't talked to her, and you always talk to her. You talk to her more than you talk to anybody."</p>

<p>Sasha looked down at her for a moment. Then she started laughing.</p>

<p>She laughed for a good deal longer than the situation called for, with her head back, holding the boards, and two people at the other end turned round, and Kamila stood there with her ears going hot and absolutely no idea what she had said.</p>

<p>"Oh, she's fine," Sasha said at last, wiping her eye with the heel of her hand. "She is completely fine. Better than fine. Do not worry about Anna Shcherbakova, little one, she is having a very productive weekend."</p>

<p>"But she's skating badly."</p>

<p>"Yes."</p>

<p>"Why?"</p>

<p>"Because she lost a bet," said Sasha, and put the bottle down, "and she's a terrible loser, and it turns out she's much worse at it than she thinks she is." She reached over and flicked the end of Kamila's plait, entirely pleased with herself. "Go and warm up properly instead of sitting on that barrier all morning counting other people. I can see you doing it, you know. Everybody can see you doing it. You're not nearly as invisible as you think you are."</p>

<p>And she was gone, back onto the ice at speed, singing.</p>

<p>Kamila stayed where she was for a while, examining the answer from several directions, and got nothing out of it at all.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p><em>Trio.</em></p>

<p>The changing room at one o'clock had gone quiet, in the way it does when the morning group has left and the afternoon group has not arrived. Somebody's speaker was still playing to nobody in the corner. The radiator ticked under the window.</p>

<p>Anna sat on the bench in front of her locker with one boot off and one boot on, holding the second one in both hands, and had been in approximately that position for eleven minutes.</p>

<p>The door banged.</p>

<p>"There she is." Sasha came in with her jacket over one shoulder and her hair still wet at the temples, in the highest of high spirits. "The three-time national champion. The pride of Russian figure skating. Do you know, Anneshka, I counted, and I want to be accurate here, because I know how you feel about numbers." She held up fingers. "Three popped jumps. One two-foot. One spin you came out of so early I thought the music had broken. And a step sequence that was, and I mean this with all my heart, the most creative interpretation of that phrase anybody has ever attempted."</p>

<p>"Go away."</p>

<p>"Absolutely not." Sasha dropped onto the bench beside her, close enough that their shoulders touched, and stretched her legs out. "I've been waiting for this all morning. Fifteen years I've been waiting to see you skate like that. It was beautiful. Eteri's face was beautiful. Somebody should have filmed it."</p>

<p>"This is your fault."</p>

<p>"My fault!"</p>

<p>"Entirely and completely your fault." Anna set the boot down and turned to look at her, and her ears were pink, and her voice was perfectly level, which under the circumstances was a considerable feat. "I have not slept. I lay awake until four in the morning. I have been trying to run a programme I have skated two hundred times, and every single time I go into the step sequence, my hands do not know where they are supposed to be, because you," and her chin came up, "held one of them for eleven minutes on a bench, and then walked me home, and then said good night as though nothing had occurred."</p>

<p>"Something occurred?"</p>

<p>"Sasha."</p>

<p>"I'm asking a genuine question. I'm not a mind reader, Anya, I'm just a girl who jumps." Sasha's grin was doing something complicated. "You could have said something at the time. You had a whole eleven minutes. You could have said, Sasha, I appear to be holding your hand, and I could have said, yes, Anya, so you are, and we could have had a really excellent conversation about it. But you didn't say anything, and I didn't say anything, and now here we are, and one of us has had a very good morning and one of us has been popping lutzes in front of the national coach."</p>

<p>"You lost me the whole day."</p>

<p>"I won you the whole day. There's a difference, and it's an important one, and it's the difference between us." Sasha leaned back against the lockers. "I'm not the one who lost the bet, my love. You broke the shadow. You went off the pattern all by yourself with nobody anywhere near you, and I've been thinking about that quite a lot since about four o'clock this morning, as it happens."</p>

<p>Anna went scarlet. It arrived from the collar upward and there was nothing whatsoever she could do about it, and she looked at the floor of the changing room as though the answer were written on it.</p>

<p>"That was a hesitation," she said. "It was one beat."</p>

<p>"It was one beat."</p>

<p>"It doesn't mean anything."</p>

<p>"Doesn't it?" said Sasha.</p>

<p>The speaker in the corner ran out of song and started another one. The radiator ticked.</p>

<p>"You've destroyed my Sunday," Anna said, to the floor. "I have a run-through at three. I have to be a person again by three o'clock, and I don't currently know how that is done, and it is entirely because of you, and you are sitting there enjoying it."</p>

<p>"I am enjoying it enormously."</p>

<p>"I know you are."</p>

<p>"Fine," said Sasha, and sat up. "Fine. Since it's my fault, and since I'm a fair person and a generous friend, let me make it up to you."</p>

<p>Anna turned her head to ask what that was supposed to mean.</p>

<p>She got about as far as the first word.</p>

<p>Sasha's hand came up along her jaw, and it was careful, unbelievably careful for a hand that spent its life throwing its owner into the air, and it turned Anna's face the last few degrees toward her. Then she leaned in and stopped, close enough that Anna could feel her breathing, and stayed there for one whole second, waiting.</p>

<p>Anna did not move away.</p>

<p>So Sasha kissed her.</p>

<p>It was slow, and it was quiet, and it was almost nothing at all for the first moment: only warmth, and a small startled sound from somewhere in the back of Anna's throat that she would deny for the rest of her natural life. Her hands had gone to the front of Sasha's jacket without asking her, and had closed there, holding on the way you hold the boards when your legs are finished. Sasha's thumb moved once along her cheekbone. The kiss went on, unhurried, without a single quad in it anywhere, and Anna Shcherbakova, who had been early or late for every single thing she had done since eight o'clock that morning, came in exactly on time.</p>

<p>When they came apart, neither of them went very far.</p>

<p>"That," Anna said, in a voice with nothing left in it, "is cheating."</p>

<p>"Everything's cheating with you." Sasha's forehead came down against hers for a moment. "You said it about the corners as well."</p>

<p>"You cut the corner."</p>

<p>"I always cut the corner, Anneshka. It's what the corners are for."</p>

<p>She got up. She picked her jacket up off the bench, and got as far as the door, and turned round in it.</p>

<p>"Next Saturday," she said. "Same terms. Except you don't have to lose anything this time, because I'm asking, and you can just say yes."</p>

<p>Anna sat on the bench with one boot off and looked at her.</p>

<p>"Nine o'clock," she said.</p>

<p>Sasha went out grinning like a dog with a stolen shoe, and the door banged behind her, and the speaker in the corner played on to nobody.</p>

<p align="center">***</p>

<p><em>Da capo.</em></p>

<p>At the three o'clock run-through, Anna Shcherbakova landed everything.</p>

<p>Kamila watched it from the boards with her water bottle finally open, counting, and found that the clock was back. Entry on the beat. Twizzle on the beat. Triple lutz, triple toe, both of them arriving exactly where the phrase asked for them, and the combination spin coming out on the last chord instead of four seconds before it. Whatever had been wrong all morning had been taken away and returned in working order, and the whole thing was so ordinary and so correct that within ten minutes nobody in the building was watching except the person who always watched.</p>

<p>She caught her at the gate afterward, because there were questions to be asked and Kamila had been storing them up since eight in the morning.</p>

<p>"That was better."</p>

<p>"Thank you, Kamilochka."</p>

<p>"You were on the beat the whole way. Even the transition into the flip, and that one's hard, that one's always a bit," Kamila made a wobbling motion with one hand, "but it wasn't today. What did you do to fix it? Was it something you thought about, or is it a thing you have to sleep off?"</p>

<p>Anna was pulling her guards on. "It fixed itself."</p>

<p>"Things don't fix themselves. You told me that. You said in October that nothing fixes itself and everything gets fixed by somebody, and you said it twice because I wasn't listening the first time."</p>

<p>"Then I was right in October," said Anna, "and I'm tired now, and you should go and get changed."</p>

<p>Kamila did not go and get changed. She stood in front of the person she had been setting her clock to for two years, and looked at her, because looking carefully was the only real talent she believed she had.</p>

<p>"Anya, you've got something on your mouth."</p>

<p>Anna stopped moving.</p>

<p>"On the side. Just there." Kamila pointed helpfully at her own face, at the corner of her own lip, demonstrating. "It's a sort of pink. I thought it was your lip balm, but your lip balm hasn't got any colour in it, I've seen it, you keep it in the front pocket of your bag."</p>

<p>"Kamila."</p>

<p>"It's the same colour Sasha wears," said Kamila.</p>

<p>The gate area went very quiet, in the specific way that only a rink can go quiet, with the plant humming somewhere under everything.</p>

<p>Kamila watched the colour arrive on the face of the most composed person she had ever met. It came up from the collar in a wave, all the way to the hairline, and it did not stop, and it did not go anywhere afterward. In two years of studying that face with an attention bordering on the criminal, she had never once seen it do that.</p>

<p>She had no idea what any of it meant. She filed it away exactly as she had found it, the way she filed everything, to be understood later.</p>

<p>"Do you want a tissue?" she asked. "I've got tissues."</p>

<p>From somewhere over by the sound desk, at the other end of the building, a laugh went up.</p>
